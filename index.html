Method & judgement

From a source export
to a structured record.

  1. Understand the brief.

    Work from the target country, sector and contact requirements. Relevance matters before volume: the company type, role and market need to match the assignment.

  2. Source and cross-check.

    Use Lusha exports for contact sourcing, then check company and role information against LinkedIn and official websites. Public sources, databases and registries support the wider research work.

  3. Prepare the data.

    Clean and manipulate records in Excel or Google Sheets. Standardise names and countries, review duplicates, organise classifications and prepare fields for CRM imports.

  4. Validate before import.

    Check the record structure, required fields and company-to-contact relationship. My work included country consistency, company classification and preparing records for a defined Pipedrive import schema.

  5. Maintain and report.

    Keep Pipedrive and ActiveCampaign information organised and reviewable. Prepare recurring reporting and research files so colleagues can follow activity and investigate exceptions.

Reporting / Design example
A dashboard starts before the chart.
A useful reporting brief defines the audience, the metric and the decision. My IN-VR experience includes Power BI dashboards and weekly KPI reporting; this is an illustrative reporting specification.
Audience
A manager reviewing research activity.
Question
What is ready for review, and where is follow-up needed?
Measures
Total records, validated records, records awaiting review, activity by market.
Checks
Define the reporting period, grain and status categories. Check for duplicate IDs and missing markets.
Views
Summary measures, market comparison, detailed exception table.
A higher record count is an activity measure. It does not, by itself, prove better data quality or stronger business results.
Excel / Method example
Build a reviewable research workbook.
My listed Excel skills include pivot tables, lookup functions, data cleaning and financial modelling. A clear workbook separates source information, checks and presentation.
  1. Source: preserve the original input and source reference.
  2. Preparation: standardise field names and formats.
  3. Validation: check missing fields and repeated identifiers.
  4. Analysis: use a pivot table to compare activity by market or status.
  5. Reporting: present a small number of defined measures with an exception list.
An example of my approach to structuring analysis, not a downloadable employer workbook.

Data preparation / SQL / Exception reporting
The decision
Prepare an import.
Keep uncertainty visible.
A team needs a company list prepared for CRM import. The useful output is a proposed standardised table and a review queue—not a claim that every populated field is correct.
Required fields
Company, country and registry identifier must be present.
Permitted changes
Trim whitespace and use explicit country aliases. Preserve the raw values.
Duplicate handling
Repeated registry identifiers are review candidates. No automatic deletion or merge.

Method
Make transformations explicit.
Whitespace is removed from field edges. ES becomes Spain; UK becomes United Kingdom; FR becomes France; DE becomes Germany. Known country spellings are standardised using a fixed mapping. Empty required fields remain missing.
Finding & decision
Review five records before import.
Records 1 and 2 share A101, so both are duplicate candidates. Record 4 lacks a registry identifier; record 5 lacks a country; record 12 lacks a company name. These five records belong in a review queue.
The other seven pass the stated completeness and uniqueness checks. They still need source verification and validation against the destination CRM’s requirements.
Limits
This example has no real company data, no measured employer impact and no automatic merging. It demonstrates the logic of preparing a dataset and making exceptions visible.

The SQL includes table creation, all twelve input rows, a normalisation view and the review query. The readme lists rules, expected results and limitations.
